\section{Related Work and Dataset Provenance}\label{sec:dataset}

Bandwidth minimization is a graph-labeling optimization problem. A
representative heuristic study is the dual-representation simulated
annealing approach by Torres-Jimenez et al.\ \cite{torres2015drsa}, which
uses 113 Harwell--Boeing benchmark instances and reports improved or
matched upper bounds. The heuristic values are not automatically
certificates of optimality, and published execution times should not
be compared directly to this project's experiments on different
hardware. Our methods instead seek exact solutions and report native
optimality evidence or a checked incumbent when the time limit expires.

The graphs in this project come from the Harwell--Boeing Sparse Matrix
Collection distributed by the NIST Matrix Market~\cite{nistmatrixmarket}.
The collection was developed by Duff, Grimes and Lewis. The project
commits an initial 12-instance subset rather than the full 113-instance
benchmark of the reference study. The corresponding
\texttt{data/HarwellBoeing/Manifest.json} records source URLs, graph
sizes and SHA-256 checksums. When converting a sparse matrix into an
unweighted, undirected graph, an off-diagonal nonzero gives an edge;
self-diagonal entries and repeated undirected directions do not add
edges, and isolated vertices are retained. All SAT, CP, and ILP runs
use the same checked \texttt{.col} graph for a given instance.
